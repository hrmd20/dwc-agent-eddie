Resource DM:

When I evaluate a patient with ED, I look beyond the symptom to understand what may be contributing. Here’s what I check:

• Blood flow and heart health: I review blood pressure, cholesterol, smoking history and cardiovascular risk. ED can sometimes be an early sign of vascular disease.

• Blood sugar: I review prior results and consider an A1c or fasting glucose test to check for diabetes.

• Hormones: I check morning total testosterone and confirm a low result on a separate morning. Depending on the findings, I may also check free testosterone, SHBG, LH or prolactin.

• Medications and supplements: I review everything the patient takes, including antidepressants, blood pressure medications and hormones. I discuss possible alternatives when appropriate—not stopping prescriptions without a plan.

• Sleep and lifestyle: I ask about sleep apnea, alcohol, nicotine, exercise and weight changes.

• Stress and sexual history: I ask when symptoms started, whether morning erections are still happening, whether the issue is consistent or situational, and how stress, mood or relationship factors may be contributing.

I combine that history with an examination and targeted testing. There isn’t one blood panel that identifies every cause, so I tailor the evaluation to each patient.

Bring your medication list and previous lab results to your appointment, and use this checklist to start the conversation with your doctor.
